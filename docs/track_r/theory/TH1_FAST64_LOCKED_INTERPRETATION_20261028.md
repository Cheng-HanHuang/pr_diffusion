## 1. Frozen-rule verdict: NEITHER wins

The 64-run primary-panel E0 prediction-interval coverage is **race 6/21 vs S2 15/21**: B22 6/7 vs 1/7, DEV80 0/7 vs 7/7, pooled 0/7 vs 7/7. The common, score-model-independent oracle forecasts also fail: O_raw 6/21 and O_orbit 4/21. ## 2. Candidate generation is the main failure, not an exchangeable residual selector

Among 25 images with no GOOD in historical runs 0-3 (B22 five, DEV80 twenty), the per-image companion predicted that 22.8 would produce GOOD within 64; **18 did**. The remaining **seven never-GOOD** images comprise one B22 image with 64/64 INCONSISTENT and six DEV80 images, of which five are all-INCONSISTENT and one (image 47283) has 29 CONSISTENT plus 35 INCONSISTENT. Six out of seven therefore fail even the observation-consistency test for every run. For DEV80, the homogeneous Dirichlet shrinkage predicts about **89%** eventual GOOD among initial zero-of-four images versus **70%** realized. This is a *tail of image-level difficulty / possible structural-zero population*, not evidence that generic per-run class exchangeability fails: the A9 exchangeability audit found no Holm-significant class-rate drift across 0-3, 4-15, and 16-63. DEV80 GOOD declined from 48.4% to 44.2% with paired p=0.056, borderline and not an established drift.

With any fixed image i, let q_i be its per-run probability of producing GOOD or a correct-orbit candidate. A finite four-run record of zero successes has likelihood (1-q_i)^4 both when q_i=0 and when q_i is merely small. The A8 calibration therefore cannot identify whether the image is **hard but solvable** or **effectively hopeless** on this solver. C-T8's Gauss-Radau moment obstruction formalizes this nonidentifiability: distinct latent image mixtures have identical four-run count distributions yet diverge at K=64. The FAST result is an empirical manifestation of that limitation; it does **not** prove mathematical q_i=0 for any finite-64 failure.

## 3. Conditional-on-generation test rescues the mechanism, not the frozen forecast

The planner's explicitly *exploratory/post-hoc* A9.2(b) calculation conditions on the observed 64-run class composition and evaluates the exact K-subset average of the selected-class winning probability under the **previously frozen** class-score rates. For pooled images at K=64 the observed E0 is **0.9389**; the primary race predicts **0.9526** (1.37 pp high), race_tau1 predicts **0.9379** (~0.10 pp low), and S2 predicts **0.8773** (~6.16 pp low). Across the K grid the primary race is 0.6-1.4 pp optimistic, the tighter-prior sensitivity within 0.3 pp, while S2 is 4-8 pp pessimistic. S2's better unconditional coverage is **error cancellation** between an overly optimistic generation model and a pessimistic class-blind selector. These conditional results explain the discrepancy but do not retroactively rescue or replace v1.

At K=64, four selection losses occur, all in DEV80, each with only 1-4 GOOD candidates among 64. Two winners are globally rotated ORBIT outputs (potentially repairable by G1), and two winners are CONSISTENT decoys. All four residual-winning margins are below 0.001 sigma. Do not infer unconditional E1 success until the actual FFHQ E1 outcome is evaluated.

## 4. Consequence for the paper and prospective follow-up

The honest central result is: **our registered prediction missed because the latent per-image generation model failed on a small hard-image stratum, while the class-conditioned residual-race explanation remained comparatively accurate after conditioning on realized composition.** Report both facts, with failure-first examples and exact frozen hashes. Preserve the distinction between measurement-inconsistent all-64 failure, measurement-consistent wrong decoys, and exact orbit ambiguity. The same images recurring across native solvers motivates an explicitly separately registered difficulty-aware v3 for as-yet withheld SITCOM/DAPS outcomes; its use of now-released FAST data must be disclosed, and it must not be described as independent of FAST. No new high-K estimate or solver rerun is justified from this note alone.
