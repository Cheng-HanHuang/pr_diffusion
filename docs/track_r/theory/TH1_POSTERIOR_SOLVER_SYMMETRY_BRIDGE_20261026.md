# TH1 supplementary result: posterior symmetry versus solver symmetry (2026-10-26)

**Status:** PROVED (abstract probability). **Novelty status:** triangle inequality, conditional independence, and relative-entropy chain rule are standard; the contribution is their *joint interpretation for Fourier-phase-retrieval prior/sampler failure anatomy*. Not a new general inequality. **Data:** none. No XP/GT/holdout files consulted.

## 1. Symmetry mismatch forces a distributional sampling error

Let G act by measurable bijections on a space X. Let `Pi_y` be the model posterior with Fourier-magnitude likelihood invariant under G. Let `L_y` be the output law of a solver, conditional on the same measurement y. All laws below may be arbitrary (there is no posterior-exactness assumption).

**Proposition 1 (symmetry-mismatch triangle bound, PROVED).** For every g in G,

    TV(Pi_y, g#Pi_y) <= 2 TV(L_y, Pi_y) + TV(L_y, g#L_y).

Consequently,

    TV(L_y, Pi_y) >= (TV(Pi_y,g#Pi_y) - TV(L_y,g#L_y))_+ / 2.

If L_y is exactly G-invariant, the inequality simplifies to `TV(L_y,Pi_y) >= TV(Pi_y,g#Pi_y)/2`.

**Proof.** By the triangle inequality and isometric invariance of TV under a measurable bijection:

    TV(Pi,g#Pi) <= TV(Pi,L) + TV(L,g#L) + TV(g#L,g#Pi)
                = 2 TV(Pi,L) + TV(L,g#L).

Rearrange and use nonnegativity.

**Interpretation.** An equivariant sampling procedure started from invariant noise produces G-invariant output. If the learned posterior assigns preferential mass to upright orbit representatives, an exactly equivariant solver cannot be simultaneously posterior-accurate without introducing prior-driven symmetry breaking. Thus denoiser non-equivariance is not intrinsically a defect in sampling: it may be essential to represent an asymmetric learned prior. What matters is whether the break matches the posterior's preference or adds algorithmic bias.

**Corollary 1 (necessary Gaussian transition defect for posterior fidelity).** If L is generated by the Gaussian Markov chain in `TH1_1_TV_BOUND_PROOF.tex` with standardized trajectory defect sum

    D_g = sum_i E_L ||m_i(X_i)-g m_i(g^{-1}X_i)||^2 / s_i^2,

then

    TV(L,Pi) >= [TV(Pi,g#Pi) - sqrt(D_g)/2]_+/2.

In particular, if `TV(L,Pi) <= eps`, then

    D_g >= 4 [TV(Pi,g#Pi)-2eps]_+^2.

**Proof.** Substitute the already proved `TV(L,g#L)<=sqrt(D_g)/2` into Proposition 1 and rearrange.

**Caution:** The bound does not give an experimentally checkable numeric certificate unless the model posterior asymmetry and an on-trajectory distributional estimate of D_g are available. XP-C2's denoiser defect on noised GT images is an interpretive proxy, **not** the D_g appearing here.

## 2. Exactly separate quotient occupancy from orientation mismatch

Let O be the orbit-valued random variable corresponding to X/G. Under the usual disintegration conditions (true on finite-dimensional Euclidean image space under finite G), every law P(dx) decomposes as P_O(do) P(dx|O=o).

**Proposition 2 (KL chain decomposition, PROVED).** If `L<<Pi` and relevant KL terms exist, then

    KL(L || Pi) = KL(L_O || Pi_O)
                + E_{o~L_O} KL(L(.|O=o) || Pi(.|O=o)).

**Proof.** Factor the Radon-Nikodym derivative into the marginal quotient density ratio times the conditional-on-orbit density ratio, take its logarithm and integrate against L.

Because the likelihood only depends on O, the target conditional term is particularly simple:

    Pi(dx|O=o,y) = pi(dx|O=o).

This identity holds for a general prior; no claim is made that prior mass is uniform over orientations. Hence there are two conceptually independent errors:

1. **Quotient occupancy:** how often the solver samples a measurement-consistent target orbit versus a wrong orbit (including the CONSISTENT and INCONSISTENT populations).
2. **Within-orbit orientation:** how its conditional orientation frequencies differ from the learned prior preference at the same orbit.

The KL equality does **not** say these are identifiable empirically from four runs or from GT labels alone, and it is **not** evidence that the solver samples an exact posterior.

## 3. Relation to the planned Track R tests

- For TH1.1, this supplements the invariant-kernel and pathwise-TV theorems with a positive reading of non-equivariance: fidelity may **require** it when the learned prior is asymmetric.
- For TH1.2, it keeps raw GOOD versus correct-orbit generation separated from verifier failures. A measurement-only selector cannot resolve exact within-orbit orientation, but a prior-dependent verifier can; that possibility is already present in CLIP and PC-DAPS rather than a novel method proposal.
- For TH2, compare class composition between DAPS's conditional sampling, SITCOM's inner optimization, and FAST-DIPS's feasibility projection as a *conditional/mechanistic question* rather than inventing theoretical ordering. Posterior exactness of the deployed methods is not asserted.
- No new solver, method, or post-freeze experiment is proposed here.

## 4. Literature and positioning

Invariant-law propagation is well established in equivariant diffusion (GeoDiff/Xu et al. 2022; Hoogeboom et al. 2022). Posterior factorization and KL chain rules are standard. The verifier ceiling is Stroebl--Kapoor--Narayanan (ICLR 2026). DAPS, SITCOM, and FAST-DIPS have method-specific guarantees that cannot be promoted to global posterior exactness. We present these elementary identities as **organizing mathematics for a prospectively tested failure taxonomy**, not as independent breakthrough theorems.
