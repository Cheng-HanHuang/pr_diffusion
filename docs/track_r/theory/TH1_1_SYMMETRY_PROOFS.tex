\section{Exact quotient structure of the Fourier observation}
\label{app:exact-quotient}

\paragraph{Measurement operator and group.}
For each channel let \(z=(x+1)/2\) on a \(256^2\) support and
\(f=Pz\) on the central \(384^2\) canvas, with support indices
\(\{64,\ldots,319\}^2\).
Let \(R_c\) rotate the original channel by \(180^\circ\) and
let \(G=\langle R_1,R_2,R_3\rangle\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3\).
No assertion is made that \(G\) exhausts all measurement ambiguities.

\begin{lemma}[Exact Fourier orbit invariance]
\label{lem:th1-orbit}
For every image \(x\) and every \(g\in G\),
\(\mathcal A(gx)=\mathcal A(x)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one channel and all canvas indices \(j,k\in\mathbb Z_{384}\),
central padding commutes with channel reversal in the form
\[
 (P R_c z)[j,k]=f[383-j,383-k]=f[-j-1,-k-1].
\]
Indeed, on the support rectangle this is precisely
\(z[255-(j-64),255-(k-64)]\), and both sides vanish off that rectangle.
Writing the two-dimensional DFT as
\(\widehat f(\omega_1,\omega_2)=
\sum_{j,k}f[j,k]e^{-2\pi i(j\omega_1+k\omega_2)/384}\),
the substitution \(j'=-j-1,\ k'=-k-1\) gives
\[
 \widehat{P R_c z}(\omega)
 =e^{2\pi i(\omega_1+\omega_2)/384}
   \widehat f(-\omega).
\]
Since \(f\) is real, \(\widehat f(-\omega)=\overline{\widehat f(\omega)}\).
Thus its absolute value is unchanged pointwise. FFT shifts and
orthonormal normalization preserve this conclusion. The operation is
independent on the three channels, establishing the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Posterior orbit ratios]
\label{prop:th1-prior-ratio}
Suppose \(p(x\mid y)=Z_y^{-1}\ell(y,\mathcal A(x))\pi(x)\),
\(Z_y\in(0,\infty)\), where \(\pi\) is a density relative to a
\(G\)-invariant reference measure. Then
\[
 p(gx\mid y)\pi(x)=p(x\mid y)\pi(gx).
\]
Where both density ratios are defined and positive this is
\(p(gx\mid y)/p(x\mid y)=\pi(gx)/\pi(x)\).
For each measurable set \(S\),
\[
 P(gS\mid y)=Z_y^{-1}
 \int_S\ell(y,\mathcal A(u))\pi(gu)\,du.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:th1-orbit} in the Bayes density, first by
cross-multiplication and then by the change of variables \(v=gu\).
The map \(g\) is a coordinate permutation and thus has absolute
Jacobian determinant one. The region formula retains the normalizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No orientation information conditional on an orbit]
\label{prop:th1-conditional-information}
Let \(X\sim\pi\) be arbitrary, let \(O=G X\) be its unordered orbit,
and suppose \(Y\mid X\sim L(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal A(X))\).
Then \(Y\perp X\mid O\). For any measurable orientation label
\(J=J(X)\), in particular one constructed from a measurable orbit
section on the set of free orbits, one has
\[
 I(J;Y\mid O)=0,\qquad P(J=j\mid Y,O)=P(J=j\mid O).
\]
Consequently, under 0--1 orientation loss, observing \(Y\)
in addition to \(O\) does not improve the conditional Bayes accuracy
\(\max_j P(J=j\mid O)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\mathcal A\circ g=\mathcal A\), it factors measurably
through the finite-group orbit map: \(\mathcal A(X)=a(O)\).
For any measurable \(B\) in observation space,
\[
 P(Y\in B\mid X,O)=L(B\mid a(O))=P(Y\in B\mid O).
\]
This is conditional independence. Apply the data processing property
of conditional independence to \(J=J(X)\); the Bayes accuracy
statement follows from maximizing the conditional class posterior.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Selection blindness and SMC qualification.}
For any score \(T(x;y)=\varphi(\mathcal A(x),y)\),
\(T(gx;y)=T(x;y)\). Thus a score-only selection rule
with a fixed orbit-invariant tie rule has the same selected
index after independent group transformations of the candidates.
Likewise, a likelihood-only particle potential \(F(\ell(y,\mathcal
A(x)))\) and residual pruning index are invariant.
This does \emph{not} imply invariance of a complete TDS importance
weight, which also includes proposal and reverse-model transition
densities. TGD incremental likelihood potentials and accelerated
likelihood pruning instantiate the narrower statement.

\begin{proposition}[Equivariant transitions preserve invariant laws]
\label{prop:th1-equivariant-chain}
Let \(G\) act orthogonally on a measurable state space and let
\(P_T=g_\#P_T\) for all \(g\in G\). Suppose the transition kernels
satisfy \(K_i(gx,gB)=K_i(x,B)\) for every \(i,g,x,B\).
Then \(P_i=g_\#P_i\) for all \(i\), especially the output \(P_0\).
If a target orbit has eight distinct members and a neighborhood
\(B_e\) has eight pairwise-disjoint translates \(gB_e\), then
\[
 P_0(X\in gB_e\mid X\in\cup_{h\in G}hB_e)=1/8
\]
whenever the conditioning event has positive probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct backwards in time:
\[
 P_{i-1}(gB)=\int K_i(x,gB)P_i(dx)
 =\int K_i(g^{-1}x,B)P_i(dx)
 =P_{i-1}(B).
\]
All translates of \(B_e\) have equal probability, and the
disjoint union has eight times the probability of any one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Native-solver scope.}
Gaussian re-noising is equivariant under coordinate permutations.
Measurement likelihood gradients are equivariant where defined.
Deterministic projections, optimizer steps, initializations, and tie
breaks must also be equivariant to invoke the proposition.
In DAPS, every Langevin microstep must be treated as a separate
stochastic transition; in SITCOM, the optimizer state and every inner
iterate must transform equivariantly; in FAST-DIPS, JVP/VJP,
finite-difference probes and line-search choices must preserve
the action. Learned denoisers are a possible, but not the only
logically possible, source of implementation non-equivariance.

\paragraph{Identifiability.}
The support width \(256\) exceeds half the \(384\) Fourier canvas,
so Hayes-type generic support-based uniqueness cannot be invoked.
All orbit conclusions are conditional on the orbit under discussion
and do not imply that it is the entire preimage of \(\mathcal A(x)\).
Classical trivial-associate theory and DAPS's eight-image display
are acknowledged as precedents.
