\section{Five-class best-of-K selection model}
Fix an image and a solver. Let independent runs have probabilities
\((p,o_g,o_p,c,u)\) for GOOD, global ORBIT, partial ORBIT, CONSISTENT,
and INCONSISTENT. Define \(q=p+o_g+o_p\), \(s=q+c=1-u\) and
\(a=(1-h)p+\tau_g o_g+\tau_p o_p\), with
\(0\le h,\tau_g,\tau_p\le1\).
S1: every INCONSISTENT run has a higher residual than every passing
run. S2: conditional on labels and the passing set, residual ranks
among passers are a uniform random permutation.
S3: the orientation post-test has K-independent class-conditional
success rates \(1-h,\tau_g,\tau_p,0\).

\begin{proposition}[Closed curves]
Under S1--S3, for every integer \(K\ge1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 O_{\rm raw}(K)&=1-(1-p)^K,&
 O_{\rm orbit}(K)&=1-(1-q)^K,\\
 E_0(K)&=[1-(1-s)^K]p/s,&
 E_1(K)&=[1-(1-s)^K]a/s.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(s=0\), the last two probabilities are defined to be zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two oracle events are unions of K independent GOOD and
GOOD-or-ORBIT events, respectively. The event of any passer has
probability \(1-(1-s)^K\). On this event S1 chooses a passer and
S2 chooses uniformly among passers. Because the independent
class labels are exchangeable, conditional on any passer its
label law is \((p,o_g,o_p,c)/s\). Multiply by the corresponding
success probabilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Imperfect-verifier correspondence]
Under S1--S2, residual argmin and an accept-first rule applied
to K independent runs have identical \emph{selected-label laws},
including the no-selection outcome if none passes. The two
rules need not choose the same physical candidate index.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on the unordered multiset of K labels, iid generation
uniformly randomly allocates the labels to chronological slots,
so the first passing label is uniform over the passing multiset.
S2 makes the residual minimum uniform over that same passing
multiset conditional on the label assignment. Marginalize.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone oracle--selector gaps]
If \(h,\tau_g,\tau_p\) do not depend on K, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Delta_K[O_{\rm orbit}-E_1]
   =q(1-q)^K-a(1-s)^K\ge0,\\
 &\Delta_K[O_{\rm raw}-E_0]
   =p[(1-p)^K-(1-s)^K]\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first gap tends to \(1-a/s\) if \(q>0\), and is identically
zero if \(q=0\). The second tends to \(1-p/s\) if \(p>0\),
and is identically zero if \(p=0\). When \(a=p+o_g\) and
\(q>0\), the first ceiling equals \((o_p+c)/s\).
These statements also hold after averaging over images.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract consecutive K curves. All signs follow from
\(0\le a\le q\le s\le1\). Treat zero-positive mass cases before
taking limits; expectation preserves monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generation versus selection failure]
For \(s>0\) put \(b=s-a\). Then
\[
 1-E_1(K)=(1-s)^K+[1-(1-s)^K]b/s.
\]
The first term decreases while the second increases to b/s.
For \(0<s<1\) and \(b>0\), their continuous equality is
\[
 K_\times=\log(b/(s+b))/\log(1-s).
\]
A discrete strict crossover is the first allowed K at which
the second term exceeds the first. For \(s=0\), failure is
always no-generation; for \(s=1\), no-generation vanishes at
K at least one; if \(b=0\), no strict crossover exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Rearrange E1 and solve
\((1-s)^K=[1-(1-s)^K]b/s\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation and limitations.}
Stroebl et al. (ICLR 2026) establish the imperfect-verifier
resampling ceiling generally. The new empirical question is
whether Fourier residual argmin satisfies S1--S2 and whether
the fitted five classes predict held-out K. Under global-only
repair, failed passing mass includes both partial ORBIT and
CONSISTENT, not only CONSISTENT. No consistent run has probability
\((1-s)^K\); no orbit-correct run has probability \((1-q)^K\).
Pairwise AUROC above one half does not guarantee better K-way
argmin selection; it is not substituted for S2.
