\section{Theory of the frozen GT-free stopping protocol}
\label{sec:suppl-protocol-theory}

\paragraph{Marked candidate law.}
Condition on an image, its measurement, and the solver. Each independent
candidate has a class $J\in\{G,O_g,O_p,C,I\}$, residual
$R=\|\mathcal A(\hat x)-y\|/\sqrt m$, threshold-pass indicator
$V={\bf1}\{R\le\tau_S\}$, and an orientation-corrected success mark $A$.
With per-class probabilities $\theta_j$ and threshold-pass rates
$v_j=\Pr(V=1\mid J=j)$,
\[
 s=\Pr(V=1)=\sum_j\theta_jv_j,\qquad r=1-s.
\]
Crucially, $v_G\simeq0.95$ when $\tau_S$ is the empirical $95$th
percentile of GOOD residuals on a matched calibration distribution.
Transferred thresholds (ImageNet FAST) need not have the same class-pass
rates. The relative class race weights fitted from unconditional
residual ranks do not themselves determine $v_j$.

\paragraph{Exact waiting-time law.}
Let $T_M$ be the index of the $M$th passing candidate and
$T=\min(T_M,N)$, with $N=N_{\max}$. For $M\le t<N$,
\[
 \Pr(T=t)=\binom{t-1}{M-1}s^M r^{t-M},\quad
 \Pr(T=N)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(N-1,s)<M\}.
\]
By the tail-sum formula,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-general}
 \mathbb E T=\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(t,M-1)}
 \binom tj s^j r^{t-j},\quad
 \Pr(\textsc{flag}_C)=r^N.
\end{equation}
For $M=2$ this is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-M2}
 \mathbb ET=\begin{cases}
 [2-Nr^{N-1}+(N-2)r^N]/s,&s>0,\\
 N,&s=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The event $\textsc{flag}_C$ means $\operatorname{Bin}(N,s)=0$;
it is \emph{not} the event $\operatorname{Bin}(N,s)<M$.

\paragraph{Success after stopping.}
Write $H_j^+$ for the probability that the residual minimum of $j$
independent candidates conditional on passing is successful after the
frozen orientation step; define $H_j^-$ similarly for nonpassing
candidates. A passing residual is no larger than any nonpassing one.
The ordered sequence of the first $M$ passing marks is iid under the
conditional passing law and independent of the waiting time, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-success-general}
 S_{M,N}=\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,s)\ge M\}H_M^+
 +\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}\binom Nj s^j r^{N-j}H_j^+ +r^N H_N^-.
\end{equation}
The deployed $M=2$ policy consequently has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-success-M2}
 S_{2,N}=(1-r^N-Nsr^{N-1})H_2^+
             +Nsr^{N-1}H_1^++r^NH_N^-.
\end{equation}
The nonpassing term need not vanish: an approximately $5\%$ tail of
GOOD candidates may be rejected by the frozen threshold. For fixed
best-of-$k$, by contrast,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-success-M2}
 F(k)=\sum_{j=1}^k\binom kj s^j r^{k-j}H_j^++r^kH_k^-.
\end{equation}
The frozen protocol interpolates $F(k)$ \emph{linearly} between adjacent
integers at $\bar K=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{images}}\mathbb E T_i$;
replacing that interpolation by $F(\bar K)$ from an exponentiated
formula would change the preregistered comparator.

For the class-based model with densities $f_j(t)$ of physical residual
and conditional orientation success $a_j(t)$, the passing-race success
term is the exact competing-risk integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-pass-race}
 H_m^+=\frac{m}{s^m}\sum_j\int_0^{\tau_S}
 a_j(t)\theta_j f_j(t)
 \Bigl(\sum_{\ell}\theta_\ell
 [F_\ell(\tau_S)-F_\ell(t)]\Bigr)^{m-1}\,dt.
\end{equation}
For zero passing candidates, $H_n^-$ has the same formula with
integration from $\tau_S$ to $\infty$ and survival probability
$\sum_\ell\theta_\ell[1-F_\ell(t)]$, normalized by $r^n$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional-pass-race} is exact under continuous
residuals; using a conditional exponential race with rates $w_j^+$
instead is an \emph{additional} surrogate assumption. The previously
frozen class-race weights $w_j$ were fitted to unconditional ranks
and do not identify the truncated residual law or the class-pass rates.

\paragraph{FLAG_C as a diagnostic.}
Let $q=\Pr(J\in\{G,O_g,O_p\})$ and
$t=\Pr(V=0,J\notin\{G,O_g,O_p\})$.
Using the full independent $N$-candidate panel to define the event
that no orbit-GOOD reconstruction exists,
\[
 \Pr(\textsc{flag}_C,\text{unsolved})=t^N,\qquad
 \Pr(\text{unsolved})=(1-q)^N.
\]
Hence the within-image precision and recall of this diagnostic are
$(t/r)^N$ and $(t/(1-q))^N$, respectively, when denominators are
positive. Heterogeneous-image precision is a ratio of expected counts,
not the mean of per-image precisions. These population calculations
are not certificates of individual reconstruction failure.

\paragraph{Exact random-order means on the frozen finite panel.}
The offline reference implementation randomly permutes an already
realized panel of $n$ reconstructions, with at most $N\le n$
sequential trials. Let $L$ of its reconstructions have $R\le\tau_S$.
For a uniform permutation, the number $J$ of passing candidates in
the first $N$ positions is hypergeometric,
\[
 \Pr(J=j)=\frac{\binom Lj\binom{n-L}{N-j}}{\binom nN}.
\]
Let $A_j^+$ be the exact finite-pool success probability of the
residual-minimum selector with orientation correction on a uniform
$j$-subset of the $L$ passing candidates, and $A_j^-$ the analogous
statistic on the $n-L$ nonpassing candidates. Sorting the passing
candidates by residual, the ordinary subset-order-statistic formula
gives
\[
 A_j^+=\sum_{r=1}^{L}a_r\frac{\binom{L-r}{j-1}}{\binom Lj},
\]
with the actual per-run post-orientation success indicators $a_r$.
Then the exact random-order success, compute, and consistency flag are
\begin{align}
 S^{\mathrm{panel}}_{M,N}
 &=\Pr(J\ge M)A_M^+
 +\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}\Pr(J=j)A_j^++\Pr(J=0)A_N^-,\\
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{order}}T
 &=\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}
   \frac{\binom Lj\binom{n-L}{t-j}}{\binom nt},\\
 \Pr_{\mathrm{order}}(\textsc{flag}_C)
 &=\frac{\binom{n-L}{N}}{\binom nN}.
\end{align}
If $N=n$, as on the three preregistered primary panels, this
simplifies to
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{order}}T
 =\begin{cases}M(n+1)/(L+1),&L\ge M,\\n,&L<M,\end{cases}
 \quad
 \Pr(\textsc{flag}_C)=\mathbf1\{L=0\},
\]
with success $A_M^+$ for $L\ge M$ and the deterministic full-panel
residual-minimum success indicator otherwise. These exact conditional
means are an independent QA of the frozen $4{,}000$-permutation
Monte Carlo, \emph{not a change} to the registered evaluator. They
must not be confused with the iid generator-level formula
\eqref{eq:stop-success-general}.

\paragraph{Equal-budget comparison: sufficient condition and limitation.}
Stochastic coupling shows that $\mathbb ET(s)$ is nonincreasing in
$s$. Therefore $\operatorname{Cov}(s,\mathbb ET(s))\le0$ in a
heterogeneous image population: the rule allocates more attempts to
images that pass the threshold less frequently. This redistribution
\emph{does not guarantee} a success advantage, since those images may
be unsolvable or the residual race may improve after more than $M$
passing candidates. If a nonpassing candidate can never be GOOD,
set $H_{\min,i}=\min_{1\le j\le M}H_{i,j}^+$ and
$H_{\max,i}=\max_{1\le j\le\lceil\bar K\rceil}H_{i,j}^+$.
Then a conservative sufficient condition for stopping to beat fixed
best-of-$K$ at the same $\bar K$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-sufficient}
 \sum_i H_{\min,i}(1-r_i^N)\ge
 \sum_i H_{\max,i}\Bigl[1-(1-a)r_i^k-a r_i^{k+1}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $k=\lfloor\bar K\rfloor$, $a=\bar K-k$.
Indeed the left side is a lower bound on protocol success by
\eqref{eq:stop-success-general}, and the right side is an upper bound
on the linearly interpolated fixed-policy success by
\eqref{eq:fixed-success-M2}.

Heterogeneity alone cannot imply domination. Take two equally likely
image types, $N=16,M=2$: the first has $s=.02$ but every passing
candidate is a wrong C; the second has $s=.90$ with half its passing
candidates GOOD and half wrong C. In the second image's within-pass
Plackett--Luce race take $(w_G,w_C)=(1,.1)$; all other candidates are
nonpassing and wrong. Exact evaluation gives
$\mathbb ET=(15.8031,2.2222)$, so $\bar K=9.01267$, but mean protocol
success is $0.35227$ versus fixed-interpolated success $0.44263$.
The rule thus loses by $9.04$ percentage points despite strongly
heterogeneous pass rates. The claim in the main text must therefore
be conditional: stopping helps \emph{when consistency is informative
and marginal extra attempts on low-$s$ images remain useful}.

\paragraph{Dependence on CONSISTENT failures.}
In a reduced G/C/I model set
$\Pr(G)=p$, $\Pr(C)=(1-p)\alpha$,
$\Pr(I)=(1-p)(1-\alpha)$. Assume every G and C passes and every I
fails, so the share of failed candidates that are CONSISTENT is
$\alpha=c/(c+u)$. Then
$s=p+c=1-u$, and the GOOD fraction among passing candidates is
$\gamma=p/(p+c)$. Under exchangeable residual ranks within the
passing group, $H_j^+=\gamma$ for all $j\ge1$ and $H_j^-=0$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:protocol-consistent-share}
 S_{2,N}=\gamma(1-u^N),\qquad
 F(k)=\gamma(1-u^k),\qquad
 \mathrm{Gain}(\alpha)=\gamma\bigl[(1-a)u^k+a u^{k+1}-u^N\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $k=\lfloor\bar K(\alpha)\rfloor$, $a=\bar K(\alpha)-k$,
and $\bar K(\alpha)$ from~\eqref{eq:stop-M2}.
This nonnegative gain tends to zero as $\alpha\uparrow1$, since
$s\uparrow1$, $\bar K\downarrow M$, and both policies use exactly
$M$ passes in the limit. The same zero-limit holds with a general
within-pass class race, although its intermediate gain need not be
nonnegative. With the actual $v_G\simeq.95$ rather than 1,
$\alpha\uparrow1$ \emph{alone} does not force $s\uparrow1$;
the limit statement requires the simplified all-G-pass model or an
explicit $s\to1$ hypothesis.

\paragraph{Reading Proposition~1 against held-out protocol results (post-outcome).}
The identities of Proposition~1 determine compute and the
zero-consistency flag from the pass probability $s$, but \emph{do
not} imply that early stopping beats equal-compute fixed-$K$:
the accuracy comparison also depends on the passing-group winner
qualities $H_j^+$. In the separately released ImageNet D1-P2 test,
stop-at-two achieved $0.514$ at $3.36$ mean runs, compared with
$0.511$ for the linearly interpolated fixed-$K$ comparator
(+$0.3$ percentage points); its three \textsc{flag}$_C$ images
included only one actually unsolved image, emphasizing that the
flag is not a failure certificate. In SITCOM B16, stopping achieved
$0.819$ at $3.29$ runs versus $0.823$ for fixed-$K$ at equal mean
runs ($-0.4$ points), even though the independently registered
rank-race forecast covered all $18/18$ E0/E1 selection points.
These held-out results are consistent with a high CONSISTENT
share making the pass event less discriminative of correctness
and with $H_j^+$ already near the fixed-$K$ selection quality,
so additional adaptive attempts convey little benefit. This
is an \emph{interpretation after evaluation}, not a fit of
$H_j^+$, a causal demonstration, or a new prospective prediction.

\paragraph{H2: a correctly predicted gain with miscalibrated levels (post-outcome).}
The DAPS F8 equal-compute protocol gain, $+3.38$ percentage points,
falls inside the \emph{prospectively committed} historical-prefix
prediction of $+2$ to $+4$ points (central $+3$), but this is
\emph{not} an accurate prediction of its component success levels:
the observed mean compute is $3.195$ rather than $2.835$ runs,
and observed stop/fixed E1 success is $(.931,.897)$, compared
with $(.957,.930)$ under exchangeable passing ranks or
$(.993,.953)$ under transferred race weights. Two sources of
model discrepancy are visible even before the held panel:
the original threshold rejected $9$ of $165$ B22 and $9$ of
$170$ DEV80 historical GOOD candidates, so the approximation
that GOOD always passes is false; and collapsing image-specific
pass probabilities $s_i$ to a cohort average loses the nonlinear
dependence of $\mathbb E T(s_i)$ and success on $s_i$.
More generally, the rank-conditioned passing winner quality
$H_j^+$ and the equal-compute fixed-$K$ comparator can both
be biased optimistically. Since the reported gain is the
\emph{difference} between those two accuracies, their
shared upward errors partly cancel even when each level
misses by roughly $3$--$6$ points. This supports the
\emph{directional} H2 stopping advantage on DAPS but
does not establish calibration of the generative or residual
ranking approximations; the frozen pre-score note is unchanged.

\paragraph{\textsc{flag}$_C$ precision under an image mixture.}
Let $U$ indicate an intrinsically unsolvable image (latent prevalence
$\pi$), and let $S$ be the image-specific probability that a run
passes the consistency threshold. Under conditional iid runs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:protocol-flag-precision}
 \Pr(U\mid\textsc{flag}_C)
 =\frac{\pi\,\mathbb E[(1-S)^N\mid U]}
 {\pi\,\mathbb E[(1-S)^N\mid U]
 +(1-\pi)\,\mathbb E[(1-S)^N\mid U^c]}.
\end{equation}
This precision can fall below $1/2$ when many solvable but low-$S$
images are flagged, while unsolvable images whose wrong reconstructions
are CONSISTENT almost never trigger the flag.
The post-hoc finite-panel analog was $5/11$ on DAPS F8 and $1/3$
on ImageNet D1-P2; \emph{no GOOD within $N$ runs} is observable with
ground truth but does not prove the latent structural impossibility $U$.

\paragraph{When increasing the cap reverses the stopping advantage (post-outcome).}
The exact waiting-time law makes this failure mechanism explicit.
Consider a mixture with a fraction $\pi_0$ of very
low-consistency-pass images (type $h$), with $s_h\ll 1/N$,
and fraction $1-\pi_0$ of ordinarily passing images (type $e$).
Write $t_j(N)=\mathbb E[T\mid j]$, $S_j(N)$ for stopped
success and $F_j(k)$ for fixed-$k$ success on type $j$.
With $\bar K_N=\pi_0t_h(N)+(1-\pi_0)t_e(N)$,
the exact equal-compute advantage is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-mixture-large-N}
 \Delta_N =
 \pi_0\{S_h(N)-F_h(\bar K_N)\}
 +(1-\pi_0)\{S_e(N)-F_e(\bar K_N)\}.
\end{equation}
Here $F_j(k)$ at noninteger $k$ means the protocol's
\emph{frozen linear interpolation}, not exponentiation
of a fresh-$k$ formula.
For two-pass stopping, $t_h(N)=N-O(N^3s_h^2)$
when $Ns_h\ll1$, while $t_e(N)$ approaches
$2/s_e$ if the cap is large relative to $1/s_e$.
Consequently the near-zero-pass component alone adds
approximately $\pi_0N$ to the average run budget.

To exhibit the mechanism without asserting that all low-pass
images are intrinsically unsolvable, suppose in a
\emph{stylized sufficient counterexample} that hard images
have $S_h(N)=F_h(k)=0$, while the easy stopped
success approaches $H^+_{2,e}$.
Then
\[
 \Delta_N\simeq (1-\pi_0)
 \{H^+_{2,e}-F_e((1-\pi_0)t_e+\pi_0N)\}.
\]
If increasing $k$ improves the easy-image fixed selector,
let $K_\star$ be a crossing
$F_e(K_\star)=H^+_{2,e}$, when it exists.
The stopping gain is negative when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop-pi0-crossing}
  \pi_0>\frac{K_\star-t_e}{N-t_e},
  \qquad N>t_e,\quad t_e<K_\star<N.
\end{equation}
Thus the effect of a near-zero-$s$ tail can increase
roughly like $N\pi_0$, while the
stopped quality on easy images saturates after the first
two passes. This condition is \emph{not} universal:
if fixed-$k$ never surpasses $H^+_{2,e}$, there
need be no crossover, and hard images may succeed via
rejected-GOOD/nonpassing outputs. Equation
\eqref{eq:stop-mixture-large-N} remains the proper
comparison in those cases.

On the released ImageNet XP-D4 completed panels,
the frozen protocol H1 fails by \emph{$-1.8$ points}
at $N_{\max}=32$ and \emph{$-3.4$ points} at
$N_{\max}=64$. In the planner's \emph{post-hoc}
budget decomposition, only four to six images
with fewer than five passing candidates consume
approximately $43\%$ of all generated runs.
The remaining images stop at $2.6$--$3.0$ runs,
while the equal-budget fixed comparator spends
$4.3$--$5.0$ on \emph{every} image.
This is evidence for the mechanism of
\eqref{eq:stop-mixture-large-N}, not a fit of
$\pi_0$, a claim that the hard images have $s=0$,
or a new preregistered forecast.
In particular, an image's repeated no-pass events
do not by themselves certify irrecoverability.

\paragraph{Why a FAST-DIPS threshold need not scale with noise (post-outcome).}
Write $y_\sigma=a+\sigma Z$ and
$d_\sigma=\mathcal A(\hat x_\sigma)-a$ for the
reconstruction's signed-measurement displacement.
The normalized residual obeys exactly
\[
 \frac{\rho_\sigma}{\sigma}=
 \frac{\|Z-d_\sigma/\sigma\|_2}{\sqrt m}.
\]
A GOOD-class residual quantile can scale linearly
with $\sigma$ only if the conditional law of
$d_\sigma/\sigma$ (including its correlation with
$Z$ and the class-selection event) is stable.
FAST-DIPS instead retains a \emph{fixed}
projection tolerance $\epsilon=0.05$:
$\epsilon/\sigma$ changes from $2.5$ at
$\sigma=.02$ to $.5$ at $\sigma=.10$.
Its returned denoised, clamped output can have
a noise-dependent fit or bias outside that
projection, so neither linearity nor a signed
direction of departure follows from the
noise-floor identity. The registered H-G1
linear extrapolations were $.02003316$ and
$.1001658$, whereas the released XP-G thresholds
are $.02223$ (about $+11.0\%$) and
$.09809$ (about $-2.1\%$), respectively;
both fail the prespecified $1\%$ criterion.
This is a \emph{post-outcome} qualification
of the earlier pre-score assessment, which had
explicitly classified H-G1 as unresolved.

\paragraph{Interacting PC-DAPS particles are not independent runs.}
PC-DAPS returns a jointly generated
\emph{bag of four interacting particles}, not
four iid samples from the single-run class model.
Although the joint law may be permutation
exchangeable under a symmetric implementation,
particle interactions invalidate the
\emph{independence} needed for the original
per-run stopping and Dirichlet extrapolations.
Choosing the residual-minimum particle and applying
the predeclared orientation test remain
ground-truth-free within a bag, regardless of
dependence. In the released \emph{descriptive}
180-image FFHQ analysis, PC-DAPS's native
particle-mean readout gives $79.0\%$ GOOD25;
E0 within the same particles gives $82.8\%$
and E1 gives $84.4\%$. These are
selection/readout comparisons on one existing
bag, not matched-NFE improvements; the
matched-cost claim was withdrawn.
A \emph{new} protocol treating each independently
generated bag as one marked candidate could
satisfy the iid \emph{bag-level} waiting-time
assumptions, after defining a label-free
per-bag residual and a cost in bags, but
no such stopping-across-bags experiment exists.
