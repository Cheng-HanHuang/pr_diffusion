\section{The protocol: registered outcomes, intervals and exploratory analyses}
\label{sec:suppl-protocol-empirical}

All numbers in this section were computed from per-image residual strings and per-candidate orientation decisions with the frozen protocol code (SHA-256 \texttt{d5f973fe}\ldots).
Ground truth enters only through the evaluation labels.
Items marked \emph{exploratory} were designed after the registered outcomes were known, use panels already seen, and change no frozen rule.

\paragraph{Registered hypotheses and outcomes.}
\Cref{tab:s4-hyp} lists every registered protocol hypothesis with its outcome.
The intervals are image-bootstrap $95\%$ intervals of the equal-mean-runs gain (5{,}000 replicates, stratified by cohort, $\tau_S$ held at its panel value; exploratory).
Their half-widths, $1$ to $3$ points, exceed H1's one-point margin, so H1's outcome on a single panel is a point-estimate statement; the gains for DAPS on FFHQ, and the loss at $N_{\max}=64$ on ImageNet, are resolved by the intervals.

\paragraph{Budget frontier and confidence-conditioned stopping (exploratory).}
On the $64$-run ImageNet panel we evaluated the frozen rule with smaller budgets, drawing runs without replacement from the $64$ available (\cref{tab:s4-frontier}).
Small budgets keep it within a point of fixed best-of-$K$; from $N_{\max}=12$ on, the loss grows with the budget, because the extra runs go to the images that never pass while the protocol's success rises only from $44.0\%$ to $49.2\%$.
We also tested one natural variant: stop only when two runs pass \emph{and} the selected output's orientation margin exceeds $c$, with $c=0.0076$, the $\textsc{flag}_O$ cut of the $16$-run ImageNet panel, fixed before the replay.
It spends more runs on images whose orientation stays uncertain and is worse at every budget from $8$ on.
We did not tune it further.

\paragraph{The label-free threshold at other noise levels (exploratory).}
The registered rule scaled FAST-DIPS's threshold linearly with $\sigma$ and failed (H-G1).
The label-free rule frozen with the protocol (median plus three scaled MADs of the per-image minimum residual over four pilot runs) needs no scaling.
\Cref{tab:s4-tau} computes it on each cohort itself (``own'') and, to charge the pilot to separate images, on the other cohort (``cross-fitted''; for DAPS-4K on ImageNet, on the other half of the images, split by a hash of the image id).
It lands within $3.5\%$ of the labeled threshold at both noise levels, and the protocol keeps or increases its gain.
The pilot costs four runs on each image of the reference set.

\paragraph{Where the ground-truth-free pick loses (exploratory).}
For each panel we split $\Oorb(K)-\Eone(K)$ with the exact subset weights of \cref{M-sec:selection}.
The selection loss is the probability that the residual pick is \cons or \incons although the $K$ runs contain an orbit-\good one; the orientation loss is the probability that the pick is orbit-\good but the output is not \good (\cref{tab:s4-decomp}).
On FFHQ the remaining gap is at most $1.1$ points for every solver.
On ImageNet, consistent-but-wrong runs that outrank a \good one cost FAST-DIPS $8$ points at $K=64$, and the generic orientation test costs $4$ to $5$ points, mostly rotations it leaves in place.
Partial orbit errors, the only kind a channel-wise correction could repair, cost at most $0.6$ points on any panel.

\paragraph{PC-DAPS on the same measurements.}
One PC-DAPS bag (four interacting particles, its own FFHQ phase-retrieval configuration) was drawn for each of the $180$ FFHQ measurements.
PC-DAPS reports the mean of its particles' metrics, the quality of a random particle; we compare it with residual selection ($\Eo$) and residual selection plus the face orientation test ($\Eone$) over the same particles (\cref{tab:s4-pcdaps}).
The difference comes from the $18$ images whose bag mixes \good and wrong particles: $135$ bags are all \good, and $27$ contain no particle that is \good in any orientation.
$\Eone$ keeps a \good particle in $17$ of the $18$ mixed bags, against $7.25$ expected for a random particle, and falls below the random-particle value on one image; the orientation test fixes three outputs and breaks none.
Because the particles interact, a bag is not four independent runs, and the stopping rule does not apply within it.
The compute labels in the lower block place the policies on a common axis; they are not a matched-compute comparison.

\paragraph{Published operating points.}
Our ImageNet ground truth and measurements are identical to those produced by the DAPS code for all $100$ images.
DAPS-4K's per-metric best of four (eight) runs gives $22.28$ ($24.07$)\,dB, SSIM $0.518$ ($0.565$) and VGG-LPIPS $0.340$ ($0.298$) on ImageNet, against $25.78$\,dB, $0.743$ and $0.254$ in the DAPS paper and $20.98$\,dB, $0.461$ and $0.387$ in the PC-DAPS rerun.
On our $180$ FFHQ images it gives $29.76$ ($30.26$)\,dB, $0.801$ ($0.811$) and $0.156$ ($0.146$), against $30.63$, $0.851$ and $0.139$ in the DAPS paper (on other images) and $29.66$, $0.770$ and $0.177$ in the PC-DAPS rerun.
These are ground-truth oracles, not ground-truth-free outputs.
