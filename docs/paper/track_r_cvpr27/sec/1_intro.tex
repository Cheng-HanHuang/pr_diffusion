\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recovering an image from the magnitude of its Fourier transform is a classical inverse problem~\cite{fienup1982phase,shechtman2015phase}.
It is ill-posed in practice: iterative algorithms stagnate in wrong solutions, and the measurement cannot distinguish an image from its $180^\circ$ rotation~\cite{fienup1986stagnation,hayes1982reconstruction}.
Diffusion models now supply the image prior for many solvers~\cite{chung2023dps,zhang2025daps,li2024difffpr,alkhouri2025sitcom,kim2026fastdips}.
Their runs still fail often.
A run may be rotated, color-scrambled, or unrelated to the image~\cite{zhang2025daps,kocabas2026pcdaps}.
The field's answer is to run each solver several times and report the best run.
In the reference code of DAPS, SITCOM and FAST-DIPS, ``best'' is decided against the ground truth, separately for each metric~\cite{zhang2025daps,alkhouri2025sitcom,kim2026fastdips}\claim{C-P1}.
The reported PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS may thus come from three different runs.
A user has no ground truth.
Which run should they keep, and when should they stop?

The natural criterion is the measurement residual, which Makris~\etal use to select runs~\cite{makris2026tgd}.
It is not obviously adequate.
A small residual does not certify a correct image.
Two of the solvers we study fit the noisy data more closely than the ground truth does, so a residual threshold cannot certify one either.
And no measurement-based rule can correct a rotated run.
How much of the published quality survives without the ground truth has not been measured.

\paragraph{What we find.}
We study DAPS, SITCOM, FAST-DIPS and PC-DAPS on shared measurements of FFHQ and ImageNet images, with up to $64$ runs per image.

\emph{Without the ground truth, selection nearly matches the oracle.}
Within an image, the residual ranks a correct run ahead of a wrong but measurement-consistent one in $200$ of $205$ pairs\claim{C-A7}.
The noise energy, shared by two runs of the same measurement, cancels in this comparison.
A zero-shot orientation test then fixes rotated picks.
Together, they nearly match the \emph{orbit oracle}, which picks with the ground truth and also undoes rotations.
On FFHQ, their share of images with a good output is within $1.1$ points of it for every solver\claim{C-A3}\claim{C-E1}.
On ImageNet, with four DAPS runs, they beat the per-metric ground-truth oracle behind the published DAPS numbers on PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS, because that oracle leaves rotated runs uncorrected\claim{C-A13}.
Choosing among the particles of PC-DAPS~\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps}, instead of averaging them, raises its share of good images from $79.0\%$ to $84.4\%$ on FFHQ and from $30.8\%$ to $53\%$ on ImageNet, at no extra cost\claim{C-X1}\claim{C-X5}.

\emph{The selected run is predictable.}
We fitted a residual-race model to the runs already observed and froze its forecasts before more runs were scored.
In five of six such tests, its intervals covered the outcome in $67$ of $69$ forecasts for the selected run, including $10$ of $10$ on images no earlier experiment had touched.
The exchangeable-ranks model of best-of-$n$ with an imperfect verifier~\cite{stroebl2026limits} covered $17$.
In the sixth test it covered more, but only through offsetting errors (\cref{sec:selection})\claim{C-F1}\claim{C-F3}\claim{C-R4}\claim{C-R5}\claim{C-R6}\claim{C-R7}\claim{C-N3}.

\emph{Whether any run is good depends on the solver and the image.}
On some images no run succeeds.
FAST-DIPS never produced a good run for $7$ of $180$ FFHQ images in $64$ runs, and for $22$ and $25$ of $100$ ImageNet images on two image sets\claim{C-F2}\claim{C-R7}\claim{C-N3}.
No selection can repair these images, and ground-truth best-of-$K$ reporting hides them.
A few runs cannot reveal them either.
With $n$ runs per image, only the first $n$ moments of the per-image success rates are identified, and these do not fix the share of images whose rate is near zero\claim{C-T8}.
Solvers also fail in different ways.
DAPS-based samplers give the best reconstructions up to rotation.
For them, residual selection loses at most $1.1$ points against the orbit oracle on either dataset; on ImageNet, the rest of the gap is orientation\claim{C-A16}\claim{C-E1}\claim{C-X3}\claim{C-X5}.
SITCOM more often returns runs that fit the measurement but are wrong: $57\%$ of its failed runs on FFHQ\claim{C-A5}.
Such runs pass any consistency check.
When no correct run lies beside them, nothing in the measurement reveals the error, so stopping cannot help.
This limit lies with the solver, outside the scope of selection.

\paragraph{A ground-truth-free protocol.}
These findings define a procedure (\cref{fig:teaser}a, \cref{sec:protocol}).
It samples until $M$ runs fit the measurement, keeps the best fit, corrects its orientation, and flags doubtful outputs.
We show exactly when stopping helps: when failed runs do not fit the measurement.
We froze the protocol and four hypotheses before evaluating it on eleven held-out panels, two of them on new images\claim{C-G1}\claim{C-N1}.
With $8$ runs on FFHQ, it gained $3.4$ and $3.9$ points over fixed best-of-$K$ at equal mean runs for two DAPS configurations.
The first gain fell inside a forecast registered before scoring\claim{C-T14}\claim{C-E5}.
Where failed runs fit the measurement, it is neutral at moderate budgets and falls behind at large ones\claim{C-N2}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Ground-truth-free selection, measured.} Residual selection with a zero-shot orientation test nearly matches the orbit oracle on FFHQ, beats the published per-metric oracle on ImageNet for DAPS, and transfers to a particle sampler (\cref{sec:audit}).
\item \textbf{Predictability.} A residual-race model of best-of-$K$ with orbit classes, exact estimators, and six prospective tests against registered forecasts, one on new images (\cref{sec:selection}).
\item \textbf{The coverage limit.} Unsolved images dominate what remains; we show why a few runs cannot identify them (\cref{sec:selection,sec:audit}).
\item \textbf{A ground-truth-free protocol} with residual selection, orientation correction, two abstention flags and sequential stopping, frozen and tested on held-out runs, with an exact account of when stopping helps (\cref{sec:protocol}).
\end{itemize}
