\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recovering an image from the magnitude of its Fourier transform is a classical, ill-posed inverse problem~\cite{fienup1982phase,shechtman2015phase}.
Diffusion models now supply the image prior for many solvers of this task~\cite{chung2023dps,zhang2025daps,li2024difffpr,alkhouri2025sitcom,kim2026fastdips}, and individual runs fail often: a reconstruction may be rotated, color-scrambled, or unrelated to the image~\cite{zhang2025daps,kocabas2026pcdaps}.
The field's response is to run each solver several times and report the best run.
In the reference implementations of DAPS, SITCOM and FAST-DIPS, ``best'' is decided against the ground truth and for every metric separately, so the reported PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS may come from three different runs~\cite{zhang2025daps,alkhouri2025sitcom,kim2026fastdips}\claim{C-P1}.
A user has no ground truth.
What should they run, which run should they keep, and when should they stop?

The natural ground-truth-free criterion is the measurement residual, and it has been used to select runs~\cite{makris2026tgd}.
It is not obviously adequate.
Phase retrieval is ill-posed, so a small residual does not certify a correct image; two of the solvers we study fit the noisy data more closely than the ground truth does, so a residual threshold cannot certify one either; and the oversampled Fourier operator cannot distinguish an image from its $180^\circ$ rotation~\cite{hayes1982reconstruction,fienup1986stagnation}, so no measurement-based rule can correct a rotated run.
How much of the published quality survives without the ground truth has not been measured, and neither has why the rest does not.

\paragraph{What we find.}
We study three published solvers (DAPS, SITCOM, FAST-DIPS) on common measurements of FFHQ and ImageNet images, with up to $64$ runs per image, and reach three conclusions.

\emph{Selection without the ground truth is close to the oracle on faces, and predictable.}
Within an image, the residual ranks a correct run ahead of a wrong but measurement-consistent one in $200$ of $205$ pairs\claim{C-A7}: the noise energy shared by two runs of the same measurement cancels in the comparison.
Residual selection followed by a zero-shot orientation test falls short of an orientation-aware ground-truth oracle by at most two images per solver and cohort on FFHQ, and by at most $1.1$ points with $8$ to $16$ runs, also among the particles of PC-DAPS\claim{C-A3}\claim{C-E1}.
For FAST-DIPS on ImageNet, where rotated runs are common, the same test closes $57$--$60\%$ of the gap between the residual pick and that oracle; at $64$ runs the remaining $13$ points split into consistent-wrong runs that outrank good ones ($8$) and errors of the generic orientation test ($5$)\claim{C-R4}\claim{C-E1}.
A residual-race model, fitted to the runs already observed and frozen before more runs were scored, covered $57$ of $59$ registered points for the selected run in four of five prospective tests; the exchangeable-ranks model of best-of-$n$ with an imperfect verifier~\cite{stroebl2026limits} covered $17$ and failed three of the four; in the fifth both overestimated generation\claim{C-R4}\claim{C-R5}\claim{C-R6}\claim{C-R7}.

\emph{The bottleneck is images the solver never solves, and they are not predictable.}
Our frozen forecasts overestimated how many images eventually yield a correct run in four of five tests, including one fitted to sixteen runs per image\claim{C-R7}.
FAST-DIPS never produced one for $7$ of $180$ FFHQ images in $64$ runs, against $2.2$ forecast, and six of the seven never produced even a measurement-consistent run\claim{C-F2}\claim{C-F5}; on ImageNet, $22$ of $100$ images had no correct run in any orientation among $64$\claim{C-R7}.
No selection can repair these images.
A few runs cannot reveal them: $n$ runs identify only $n$ moments of the per-image success distribution, and its mass near zero is not among them\claim{C-T8}.
And ground-truth best-of-$K$ reporting hides them.

\emph{Measurement-based selection is orientation-blind, and a cheap test fixes it.}
The symmetry is classical; its consequence for selection is that the within-orbit choice must come from the prior, and a zero-shot CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} test fixes all $58$ global rotations among $1{,}960$ FFHQ runs without breaking a correct one\claim{C-A2}.

\paragraph{A ground-truth-free protocol.}
These findings define a procedure (\cref{fig:teaser}a, \cref{sec:protocol}).
It draws runs until $M$ of them reach the solver's own consistency level, keeps the run with the smallest residual, and corrects its orientation; it flags images on which no run becomes consistent and outputs whose orientation the test is unsure of.
Each component is simple; what we add is evidence for each, a calibration that transfers across datasets, an exact account of what stopping costs and buys, and a mechanism for where it helps: where failures are inconsistent rather than consistent-but-wrong.
We froze it, with four falsifiable hypotheses, before evaluating it on nine held-out panels\claim{C-G1}.
With $8$ to $16$ runs it never fell more than one point below fixed best-of-$K$ at equal mean runs, and gained $3.4$--$3.9$ points for DAPS on FFHQ, as a theory note had predicted before scoring; where failed runs fit the measurement it was neutral within sampling error, and with $32$--$64$ runs on ImageNet it fell behind, because a few images that never produce a consistent run absorb the budget.
The same selection also improves the concurrent PC-DAPS~\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps}: choosing among its particles, instead of reporting their average quality, raises its share of good images from $79.0\%$ to $84.4\%$ at no extra cost\claim{C-X1}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Predictability of ground-truth-free selection.} A residual-race model of best-of-$K$ with orbit classes, exact estimators, and five prospective tests against forecasts registered before the data, including a failure (\cref{sec:selection}); the selection transfers to a particle sampler (\cref{sec:audit}).
\item \textbf{The coverage limit.} Unsolved images dominate what remains, forecasts miss them even from sixteen pilot runs, and we bound what a few runs can identify about them (\cref{sec:selection,sec:audit}).
\item \textbf{A ground-truth-free best-of-$K$ protocol} with residual selection, orientation correction, two abstention flags and sequential stopping, frozen and tested on held-out runs, with an exact account of when stopping helps and evidence that it does only where failed runs are inconsistent (\cref{sec:protocol}).
\item \textbf{A re-evaluation} of three published solvers on two datasets that separates the solver, ground-truth-free selection and the ground-truth oracles, with reporting recommendations (\cref{sec:audit,sec:discussion}).
\end{itemize}
