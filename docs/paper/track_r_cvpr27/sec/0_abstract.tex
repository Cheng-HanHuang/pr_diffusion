\begin{abstract}
Diffusion-prior solvers for Fourier phase retrieval are evaluated by running each method several times and reporting, for every metric separately, the run closest to the ground truth.
We ask how much of this quality is available without the ground truth, and why the rest is not.
For three published solvers on FFHQ, keeping the run with the smallest measurement residual and correcting its orientation with a zero-shot test falls short of an orientation-aware ground-truth oracle by at most two images per solver and cohort; for FAST-DIPS on ImageNet it closes $57$--$60\%$ of the gap.
Which run to keep is predictable: in five tests against forecasts registered before the runs were scored, a residual-race model predicted the selected run's success at $57$ of $59$ points in four tests, against $17$ for the exchangeable-ranks model of best-of-$n$, and both missed in the fifth.
Whether any run is good is not: the forecasts overestimated coverage in four of the five tests, even when fitted to sixteen runs per image, and $22\%$ of ImageNet images had no good run in $64$.
We turn these findings into a ground-truth-free protocol that samples until two runs are measurement-consistent, selects by residual, corrects orientation, and flags uncertain outputs.
Frozen before its evaluation on seven held-out panels with $8$ to $16$ runs, it never fell more than one point below fixed best-of-$K$ at equal compute, and gained $3.4$--$3.9$ points for DAPS on FFHQ, whose failed runs rarely fit the measurement, as predicted before scoring; with $32$--$64$ runs on ImageNet it fell $1.8$--$3.4$ points behind, spending its budget on images that never become consistent.
We re-report published results under it.
\end{abstract}
