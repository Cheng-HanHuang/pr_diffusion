\begin{abstract}
Diffusion-prior solvers for Fourier phase retrieval are run several times per image, and reported numbers keep, per metric, the run closest to the ground truth.
A user has no ground truth.
We measure how much of the quality survives without it.
Our pick keeps the run with the smallest measurement residual and corrects its orientation with a zero-shot test.
On FFHQ, its share of good images is within $1.1$ points of an orientation-aware ground-truth oracle, for four solvers.
On ImageNet, with four DAPS runs, it recovers $80\%$ of that oracle's PSNR gain over the first run and scores above the per-metric oracle of the same runs.
On PC-DAPS's particles, it raises the share of good images from $79.0\%$ to $84.4\%$ on FFHQ and from $30.8\%$ to $53\%$ on ImageNet, at no extra cost.
In six prospective tests, a residual-race model forecast the selected run with a mean interval score of $30$ points, against $182$ for the exchangeable-ranks model of best-of-$n$; it lost two tests, one clearly.
Phase retrieval is ill-posed: with FAST-DIPS, $22$--$25\%$ of ImageNet images had no good run in $64$.
No selection among the runs can recover such images, and a few runs cannot reveal them.
Our ground-truth-free protocol samples until two runs fit the measurement, keeps the best fit, corrects its orientation and flags uncertain outputs.
Frozen before evaluation, it beat fixed best-of-$K$ at equal mean runs by $3.4$ and $3.9$ points for two DAPS configurations, on fresh runs of known images.
Where failed runs also fit the measurement, it did not help.
\end{abstract}
