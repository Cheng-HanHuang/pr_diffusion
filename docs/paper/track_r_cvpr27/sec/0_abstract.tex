\begin{abstract}
Diffusion-prior solvers for Fourier phase retrieval are usually evaluated by running each method several times and reporting, for every metric separately, the run closest to the ground truth.
We ask what this protocol measures and what is achievable without the ground truth.
First, the standard oversampled Fourier operator has an exact eight-element symmetry group (per-channel $180^\circ$ rotations).
We prove that every selector and every particle weight computed from the measurements is blind to it, and that the orientation a guided diffusion sampler picks is determined solely by the denoiser's lack of equivariance, which we bound in total variation and measure.
Second, we cast best-of-$K$ selection as resampling with an imperfect verifier, derive closed-form $K$-curves, exact estimators and a gap to the oracle that never shrinks with $K$, and test predictions frozen before the data up to $K{=}64$.
Third, we audit \pending{three} solvers on FFHQ and ImageNet.
On FFHQ, selecting by measurement residual and then applying a generic orientation test recovers \pending{89--97\% (prior-based orientation; E1\_CLIP value from R0-N.2)} of the gain of a ground-truth oracle that may also correct orientation, and most remaining failures are runs that never come close to the image.
\pending{ImageNet and $K$-scaling results (XP).}
We end with reporting recommendations.
\end{abstract}
