\section{A ground-truth-free protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{The rule.}
The protocol takes the measurement $y$, a solver $S$ with a consistency threshold $\tau_S$, an orientation scorer $u$, a target $M$ and a budget $N_{\max}$ (\cref{fig:teaser}a).
(1)~Draw runs $\hat x_1,\hat x_2,\dots$ of $S$ with fresh seeds, and stop as soon as $M$ of them have $\rho\le\tau_S$, or after $N_{\max}$ runs.
(2)~Keep $\hat x=\argmin_j\rho(\hat x_j)$.
(3)~Output $R_{111}\hat x$ if $\delta=u(R_{111}\hat x)-u(\hat x)>0$, and $\hat x$ otherwise.
(4)~Raise $\textsc{flag}_C$ if no drawn run had $\rho\le\tau_S$, and $\textsc{flag}_O$ if $|\delta|$ falls in the lowest $30\%$ of the margins of the evaluated outputs, a batch-level triage rule rather than a calibrated confidence.
We use $M=2$ throughout.
No step uses the ground truth.
Its overhead, one FFT per run and $8.5$\,ms of orientation test per output, is negligible against $30.6$\,s for a PC-DAPS sample or $66.0$\,s for a DAPS-4K run\claim{C-A2}\claim{C-X2}; within a solver, equal mean runs thus means equal compute.

\paragraph{The residual compares runs; it does not certify them.}
Within an image, a \good run has a smaller residual than an \incons run in $570$ of $571$ pairs, and than a \cons run in $200$ of $205$\claim{C-A7}.
Two runs of the same image are scored against the same noise $\eta=\sigma n$.
With $e_j=\cA(\hat x_j)-\cA(\xs)$, we have $m(\rho_i^2-\rho_j^2)=\|e_i\|^2-\|e_j\|^2-2\langle\eta,e_i-e_j\rangle$: the noise energy $\|\eta\|^2$ cancels, and a cross term with the difference of the two fits remains.
Since the runs depend on $\eta$, this motivates the comparison but does not guarantee it; the evidence is empirical, also per image (\cref{sec:selection}).
A \cons run of SITCOM fits worse than a \good run of the same image by a median of only $0.7\%$ of $\sigma$, yet the order is systematic\claim{C-A8}.
Absolute residual levels, in contrast, are set by the solver.
With signed measurements, the ground truth has $\rho(\xs)=\sigma$ to within $0.3\%$.
The \good runs of DAPS-2K and SITCOM, whose data terms are overweighted (\cref{sec:setup}), have median residuals of $0.946\sigma$ and $0.884\sigma$, against $0.997\sigma$ for FAST-DIPS\claim{C-A6}.
A threshold at the noise level would accept most of their failed runs, and one fitted to their \good runs would reject the ground truth.
The protocol therefore uses $\tau_S$ only to decide when enough candidates exist, and when none does.
$\textsc{flag}_C$ means that no run reached the solver's own consistency level, not that the output is wrong.

\paragraph{Calibrating $\tau_S$ once per solver.}
We set $\tau_S$ to the threshold $\tau$ of \cref{sec:setup} for solver $S$.
It behaves as a constant of the solver and noise level.
For FAST-DIPS it is $0.0501$ on FFHQ and $0.0502$ on ImageNet, and for DAPS-2K and SITCOM it varies by under $0.3\%$ across our FFHQ cohorts\claim{C-G3}.
We therefore calibrate it once on images with ground truth and use it unchanged elsewhere; on ImageNet, the protocol uses the FFHQ value.
Where no calibration set exists, a label-free threshold can replace it: the median plus three scaled median absolute deviations of the per-image minimum residual over a pilot of four runs.
It lands within $2\%$ of $\tau$ for every solver and cohort\claim{C-G3}.
With pilots of two or three runs, it stays within $2.4\%$ at $\sigma=0.05$ (supplement)\claim{C-V2}.
The two DAPS-4K panels of \cref{tab:protocol} use this label-free threshold, so no step on them uses a label\claim{C-G9}; their pilot reuses the first four evaluated runs, a one-time cost not counted in the mean runs.
Selection does not use $\tau_S$, but stopping needs it to within about $1\%$ for DAPS, whose residuals are tightly concentrated.
Labeled, label-free and cross-cohort thresholds lie within $0.4\%$ of each other and give gains of $+2.6$ to $+5.0$ points on FFHQ; a threshold $2\%$ too low never stops and flags every image, and one $2\%$ too high keeps $+0.7$ to $+1.9$ points (supplement)\claim{C-V4}.

\paragraph{Orientation.}
By \cref{sec:setup}, the choice within an orbit must come from the prior.
On FFHQ, a zero-shot CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} test with fixed upright and upside-down face prompts fixes all $58$ global orbit errors among $1{,}960$ runs and flips none of the $1{,}171$ \good runs\claim{C-A2}.
On ImageNet, the generic prompts ``a photo.''\ and ``an upside-down photo.''\ repair $57$ of $67$ global orbit errors of FAST-DIPS and flip $5$ of $100$ \good runs\claim{C-A11}.
Its margin is informative: development outputs outside the least confident $30\%$ are \good in $65.3\%$ of cases, against $50.7\%$ for all\claim{C-G4}.

\paragraph{What stopping buys, and when.}
Fixed best-of-$K$ spends $K$ runs on every image; the stopping rule spends few on an image whose runs are usually consistent, and $N_{\max}$ on one whose runs never are.
\begin{proposition}[stopping at two consistent runs]
\label{prop:stop}
Fix an image whose runs pass $\rho\le\tau_S$ independently with probability $s$, and let $r=1-s$.
Let $H_j^+$ ($H_N^-$) be the probability that residual selection and the orientation test return a \good image from $j$ passing ($N$ non-passing) runs.
With $M=2$ and budget $N_{\max}=N$, the number of runs $T$ satisfies
\begin{align*}
\E T&=\bigl(2-Nr^{N-1}+(N-2)r^N\bigr)/s,\\
\Prob(\textsc{flag}_C)&=r^N,\\
\Prob(\mathrm{success})&=(1-r^N-Nsr^{N-1})\,H_2^+\\
&\quad+Nsr^{N-1}H_1^+ + r^NH_N^-.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}
The proof rests on one fact: the first two passing runs are independent draws from the passing law, whenever they arrive (supplement)\claim{C-T12}.
The consistency rate $s$ thus decides how many runs are spent.
How often a consistent run is correct, $H^+$, decides what they buy.
The proposition is a decomposition, not a comparison with fixed best-of-$K$; heterogeneity of $s$ alone does not make stopping better (supplement, counterexample).
Empirically, stopping gained when consistency predicted correctness, \ie when failed runs were \incons.
On the same $80$ images of cohort~B with four runs each, it gains $4.9$ points over fixed best-of-$K$ at equal mean runs for DAPS-2K, whose failed runs there are $12\%$ \cons.
For SITCOM, whose failed runs are $60\%$ \cons, it gains $-0.1$ points\claim{C-G2}.

\paragraph{Development results.}
Runs of an image are exchangeable in our data\claim{C-F4}, so random orders of an image's runs simulate sequential sampling.
The protocol's value then has an exact subset form, like the baselines of \cref{sec:selection}\claim{C-T13}.
For FAST-DIPS on FFHQ with $64$ runs per image (\cref{fig:teaser}b), the protocol with $N_{\max}=64$ returns a \good image for $91.3\%$ of the $180$ images at $8.4$ runs per image.
Fixed best-of-$K$ reaches $89.1\%$ at the same mean number of runs, and $91.2\%$ with $16$ runs\claim{C-G1}.
The protocol beats fixed best-of-$K$ by $0.8$ to $4.7$ points at all $12$ operating points we tried ($M\le4$; $\tau_S$ scaled by $0.997$ to $1.003$).
$\textsc{flag}_C$ is raised for $8$ images, $6$ of the $7$ that FAST-DIPS never solves; the seventh yields \cons runs\claim{C-G1}\claim{C-F5}.
These numbers are in-sample: we designed the protocol on these runs.

\paragraph{Frozen held-out test.}
We froze the protocol, its parameters and four hypotheses before any held-out per-image result existed\claim{C-G1}.
Before generating the panels added later, we extended the hypotheses to them (\cref{tab:protocol}).
The last panels use ImageNet$'$\claim{C-N1}.
H1: at equal mean runs, the protocol loses at most one point against fixed best-of-$K$ (point estimate, as for H2).
H2: it gains at least two points on DAPS-2K, and more than on SITCOM and on FAST-DIPS on ImageNet, whose failed runs are mostly \cons.
H3: at least half of the $\textsc{flag}_C$ images are unsolved.
H4: on ImageNet, outputs without either flag are \good at least five points more often than all outputs without $\textsc{flag}_C$.

\paragraph{Outcome.}
With $8$ to $16$ runs, H1 holds on seven of eight panels: six in \cref{tab:protocol} and two at other noise levels (below)\claim{C-G5}\claim{C-G6}\claim{C-G7}\claim{C-G9}\claim{C-N2}.
The protocol gains where failed runs do not fit the measurement.
On FFHQ, it gains $3.4$ points for DAPS-2K and $3.9$ for DAPS-4K, with image-bootstrap $95\%$ intervals above $+2$ points\claim{C-E5}.
For DAPS-2K it comes within $0.8$ points of best-of-$8$ with $40\%$ of the runs.
H2 holds.
A theory prediction registered before the DAPS-2K panel was scored had forecast a gain of $2$ to $4$ points from the historical runs alone, while overestimating the success levels by $3$ to $6$ points\claim{C-T14}.
Where failed runs fit the measurement (SITCOM, and FAST-DIPS and DAPS-4K on ImageNet), the gain lies between $-1.5$ and $+0.3$ points, within intervals of $\pm2$ to $\pm3$, so these panels are inconclusive.
Stopping there moves along the fixed-$K$ curve, trading accuracy for compute.
At this budget H1 fails once, on ImageNet$'$ ($-1.5$ points at $N_{\max}{=}16$), a loss that one panel cannot resolve either way\claim{C-E5}\claim{C-N2}.
With $32$ and $64$ runs on ImageNet, H1 fails, as registered for ImageNet$'$: the protocol falls $1.8$ and $3.4$ points behind, and $3.1$ on ImageNet$'$.
The few images whose runs almost never pass absorb $40$--$43\%$ of all runs, while the others stop after about three runs and would gain from more; this is the counterexample of the supplement, in data\claim{C-G8}\claim{C-N2}.
Smaller budgets keep it within a point, and requiring a confident orientation before stopping does not help (exploratory, supplement)\claim{C-E2}\claim{C-E3}.
H4 holds at all five ImageNet budgets ($+10.7$ to $+13.6$ points)\claim{C-N2}.
H3 failed on the three panels where it was registered ($1$ of $3$, $5$ of $11$ and $1$ of $3$ flagged images unsolved), so $\textsc{flag}_C$ marks hard images rather than unsolved ones.
At $\sigma=0.02$ and $0.10$, the label-free threshold fitted on the other cohort's pilots lands within $3.5\%$ of the calibrated one, and with it the protocol gains $4.1$ and $0.9$ points (exploratory)\claim{C-E4}.
Scaling $\tau_S$ linearly with $\sigma$ fails instead (supplement)\claim{C-G10}.
