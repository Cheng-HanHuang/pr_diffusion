\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diffusion priors for phase retrieval.}
DPS~\cite{chung2023dps} guides a pretrained diffusion model with the gradient of the measurement likelihood at the denoised estimate.
DAPS~\cite{zhang2025daps} decouples consecutive steps by re-noising a measurement-corrected clean estimate.
DiffFPR~\cite{li2024difffpr} targets oversampled Fourier phase retrieval directly.
SITCOM~\cite{alkhouri2025sitcom} enforces measurement and diffusion consistency at every step by optimizing the network input; its authors' phase-retrieval code, which we run, instead follows each ODE denoising step with a measurement-gradient step and refines the estimate by Langevin dynamics\claim{C-S6}.
FAST-DIPS~\cite{kim2026fastdips} uses analytic steps with a hard measurement constraint.
For phase retrieval, DPS, DAPS, SITCOM and FAST-DIPS report a best-of-four protocol\claim{C-P1}\TODO{check DiffFPR's protocol}.
PC-DAPS~\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps} replaces independent runs by interacting particles pruned by prior consistency, which reduces orientation failures, especially on FFHQ.
TGD~\cite{makris2026tgd} runs particles through tempered posteriors; for phase retrieval its accelerated variant keeps the lowest-residual of four partial DAPS trajectories~\cite[Tab.~2]{makris2026tgd}.
Asymptotically exact samplers exist under idealized assumptions~\cite{wu2023tds,makris2026tgd}, and the failure modes of approximate posterior samplers have been analyzed in exactly solvable settings~\cite{burns2026whenwhy,dandapanthula2026tilting}.
The phase-retrieval settings of DAPS, SITCOM and FAST-DIPS weight the data more strongly than the noise level implies (\cref{sec:setup}); the classical discrepancy principle instead fits the data only to the noise level~\cite{morozov1966discrepancy}, and a solution fitted to noisy data has a smaller expected residual than the truth, by $\sigma^2$ per fitted degree of freedom~\cite{stein1981sure}.

\paragraph{Evaluation practice.}
The ground-truth best-of-four protocol originates in DPS~\cite{chung2023dps}; DAPS adopts it ``due to the inherent instability of phase retrieval''~\cite[Sec.~4.1]{zhang2025daps}.
In the DAPS and FAST-DIPS reference code, the best run is chosen separately for each metric~\cite{zhang2025daps,kim2026fastdips}\claim{C-P1}.
Makris~\etal\cite[App.~F.4]{makris2026tgd} point out that this oracle is unavailable at test time and select by measurement error instead; they find that residual-selected best-of-four DAPS is a strong baseline at matched compute~\cite[Tab.~4]{makris2026tgd}.
Kocabas~\etal\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps} show that the ground-truth oracle hides orientation failures.
We quantify each layer of the protocol across solvers, datasets and metrics, explain the residual's blindness to orientation, and predict how the layers scale with the number of runs.

\paragraph{Classical phase retrieval.}
In two or more dimensions, generic images are determined by their Fourier magnitudes up to translation and conjugate inversion~\cite{bruck1979ambiguity,hayes1982reconstruction}, provided the support occupies at most half the grid in each dimension~\cite{barnett2020geometry}; the standard diffusion benchmark canvas does not satisfy this condition (\cref{sec:setup}).
Iterative algorithms are equally likely to reconstruct an object or its twin, and stagnate in mixtures of both when the support is centrosymmetric~\cite{fienup1986stagnation}.
Coherent diffraction imaging has long run many random starts and kept or combined them according to their Fourier-space error~\cite{chen2007ghio}\TODO{verify the GHIO selection rule in the paper}; it also averages aligned runs, with the phase retrieval transfer function measuring their reproducibility~\cite{chapman2006highres}.
Our measurement-based selector is of this kind; what is new is its analysis under a learned prior.

\paragraph{Best-of-\texorpdfstring{$n$}{n} and verifiers.}
Unbiased estimators of best-of-$k$ success from $n$ samples are standard in code generation~\cite{chen2021codex}.
Stroebl~\etal\cite{stroebl2026limits} show that a verifier with false positives caps the accuracy of resampling, with heterogeneous task difficulty making the cap bite early.
Yano~\etal\cite{yano2026tail} show that the large-$N$ behavior of best-of-$N$ is decided by the competition between the score tails of correct and incorrect samples, so accuracy can rise, saturate or fall with $N$.
Dandapanthula and Boffi~\cite{dandapanthula2026tilting} prove that finite-particle guidance cannot choose between modes that the reward gradient does not distinguish, whereas best-of-$n$ with a mode-resolving reward can.
In our setting the measurement residual is exactly such a non-resolving reward for the orbit.
The invariance of marginals under equivariant Markov kernels is used in equivariant generative modeling~\cite{xu2022geodiff,hoogeboom2022edm}; we apply it to an inverse problem whose forward operator is symmetric.
