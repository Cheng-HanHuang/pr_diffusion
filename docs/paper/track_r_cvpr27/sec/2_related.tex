\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diffusion priors for phase retrieval.}
DPS~\cite{chung2023dps} guides a pretrained diffusion model with the gradient of the measurement likelihood at the denoised estimate.
DAPS~\cite{zhang2025daps} decouples consecutive steps by re-noising a measurement-corrected clean estimate.
DiffFPR~\cite{li2024difffpr} targets oversampled Fourier phase retrieval.
SITCOM~\cite{alkhouri2025sitcom} enforces measurement and diffusion consistency at every step; its phase-retrieval code follows each ODE denoising step with a measurement-gradient step and refines the estimate by Langevin dynamics\claim{C-S6}.
FAST-DIPS~\cite{kim2026fastdips} uses analytic steps with a hard measurement constraint.
PC-DAPS~\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps}, concurrent with this work, replaces independent runs by interacting particles pruned by prior consistency, which reduces orientation failures.
TGD~\cite{makris2026tgd} runs particles through tempered posteriors.
DOLPH~\cite{shoushtari2022dolph} and DPnP~\cite{xu2024dpnp} apply diffusion priors to coded-mask measurements, whose random masks remove the twin ambiguity~\cite{candes2015cdp}, as known references do~\cite{barmherzig2019holographic}; we keep the standard operator, which has it.
DDRM-PR~\cite{kaya2025ddrmpr} and I2I-PR~\cite{kaya2026i2ipr} refine HIO reconstructions with diffusion models, and FlowSGS~\cite{li2026flowsgs} uses a flow prior.

\paragraph{Evaluation practice.}
The ground-truth best-of-four protocol originates in DPS~\cite{chung2023dps}.
DAPS adopts it ``due to the inherent instability of phase retrieval''~\cite[Sec.~4.1]{zhang2025daps}.
In the DAPS, SITCOM and FAST-DIPS reference code, the best run is chosen separately for each metric\claim{C-P1}.
Makris~\etal note that this oracle is unavailable at test time and select by measurement error instead~\cite[App.~F.4]{makris2026tgd}.
Kocabas~\etal show that it hides orientation failures~\cite{kocabas2026pcdaps}.
FlowSGS reports the best of eight posterior samples~\cite[Sec.~4.3]{li2026flowsgs}.
DDRM-PR and I2I-PR keep the HIO starts with the smallest residual and resolve the twin ambiguity against the ground truth~\cite{kaya2025ddrmpr,kaya2026i2ipr}.
We measure each layer of the protocol, model how selection scales with the number of runs, and replace fixed best-of-$K$ by a stopping rule.

\paragraph{Best-of-$n$, verifiers and adaptive sampling.}
Unbiased estimators of best-of-$k$ success from $n$ samples are standard in code generation~\cite{chen2021codex}.
A verifier with false positives caps the accuracy of resampling~\cite{stroebl2026limits}.
The large-$n$ behavior of best-of-$n$ is decided by the score tails of correct and incorrect samples~\cite{yano2026tail}.
Stopping once samples agree saves compute in self-consistency decoding~\cite{aggarwal2023adaptive,li2024escape}.
Language models also allocate test-time compute by input difficulty~\cite{snell2025scaling,damani2025hard}; our rule allocates by observed consistency, with no learned difficulty predictor.
Abstaining on low-confidence inputs is the subject of selective prediction~\cite{geifman2017selective}.
Coherent diffraction imaging has long run many random starts and kept or combined them by their Fourier-space error~\cite{chen2007ghio,chapman2006highres}; our protocol belongs to this family.
What is specific to phase retrieval with a learned prior is threefold: the verifier is a residual whose level is set by the solver, it is blind to an exact symmetry, and its failures split into kinds with different remedies.
