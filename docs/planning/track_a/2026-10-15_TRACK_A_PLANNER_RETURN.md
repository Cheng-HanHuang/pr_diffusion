## A0. Native operator, internal objective, and selector audit

### Shared observation model (with a critical discrepancy)

DAPS and the pinned SITCOM-ODE operator both map `x∈[-1,1]` to `(x+1)/2`, symmetrically pad at `int((oversample/8)*256)=64` for oversampling=2, and return **channelwise FFT magnitude** (nonnegative). Gaussian observation data are signed: `y_raw=|FFT(pad(x_true))|+N(0,0.05² I)`. Each native operator defines the summed squared residual `L_y(x)=||A(x)-y_raw||²_2` and log likelihood proportional to `-L_y(x)/(2 σ_y²)`.

Sources: [DAPS operator and likelihood](https://github.com/zhangbingliang2019/DAPS/blob/e7a77d094167084faed19b599b96673b7bb11447/forward_operator/__init__.py#L75-L113), [DAPS PR](https://github.com/zhangbingliang2019/DAPS/blob/e7a77d094167084faed19b599b96673b7bb11447/forward_operator/__init__.py#L300-L316), [SITCOM operator/likelihood](https://github.com/sjames40/SITCOM_ODE/blob/275ab67efbd8146bffca20155171ba6be1169c09/forward_operator/__init__.py#L35-L57), [SITCOM PR](https://github.com/sjames40/SITCOM_ODE/blob/275ab67efbd8146bffca20155171ba6be1169c09/forward_operator/__init__.py#L244-L259).

But **historical NP changes its observation**: B22 and B24 verify `y_raw` against the manifest then feed `y_plus=clamp_min(y_raw,0)` to *both scoring and amplitude projection*. DAPS and SITCOM feed signed `y_raw` to the principal data-fitting likelihood; the B20 DAPS auxiliary low-frequency projection may separately use `abs(y)`, not in its likelihood. B25 found negative observations in all 80 DEV80 images; median relative L2 clamp difference 0.08223 (range 0.05596–0.14793). This is **proven preprocessing mismatch, not proven causal quality difference**. B26 alone evaluates H/R. See [B25 final audit](../../b25/B25_POSTRUN_NATIVE_NP_AUDIT.md), [B22 NP worker](../../../scripts/b22/run_b22_2_np_worker.py), [B24 DEV80 NP wrapper](../../../scripts/b24/run_b24_3_dev80_np.py#L88-L101).

### Four different selection concepts—not interchangeable

1. **Publication best-of-four**: The [DAPS paper v3, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01521v3) explicitly says PR reports best of four independent samples, with oversampling two. The [SITCOM paper v2, Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04479v2) explicitly does likewise for SITCOM and all its baselines. These are *offline evaluation* conventions, not a deployable quality selector.
2. **Ground-truth oracle**: In the B24 code, `argmax_{j≤K} raw_RGB_PSNR(x_j,x_true)` with earliest-replicate ties. Per-candidate SSIM (higher) and LPIPS (lower) are auxiliary; B24 *does not* optimize each metric separately when choosing the PSNR oracle. DAPS upstream evaluator defaults to PSNR first and uses `argmax` for higher-is-better or `argmin` for lower-is-better main evaluation metric in its optional FID best-sample selection. Whether all numbers in the original papers use identical per-image PSNR-oracle selection and 8-bit output quantization is **not explicitly certified by prose or code**: confirm before claiming exact metric-wise reproduction. [B24 evaluator](../../../scripts/b24/evaluate_b24_baseline_image.py#L109-L159); [DAPS upstream best-sample branch](https://github.com/zhangbingliang2019/DAPS/blob/e7a77d094167084faed19b599b96673b7bb11447/posterior_sample.py#L249-L264).
3. **Frozen executable selector**: DAPS Fresh2 margin, SITCOM correction norm, NP post-projection LF MSE. All operate without GT and are algorithm/trajectory-specific.
4. **Common external selector**: a candidate-only, GT-free statistic evaluated *identically* on final images and the **same raw signed observation**—for example `L_y(x)`—can be a diagnostic across families but is *not* necessarily the best deployment rule for any one family. It must be preregistered/held out, not selected by its B22/B24 PSNR correlation. Include computation of the external score itself in accounting.

**Selection fairness principle:** do **not** add DAPS's `(x-x0hat)` Gaussian approximation, SITCOM's stepwise consistency, and NP's LF/S2 score into one pseudo-unified objective. DAPS and SITCOM `x0hat` and `sigma_t` are trajectory/time dependent, so their inner regularizers are not general comparable posterior energies at completed outputs. Comparing final `L_y` is statistically meaningful under identical raw measurement and normalization but measures data fit *only*, not image prior or perceptual plausibility. Exact-fit ambiguity (180° reversal, ghost/twin modes) makes residual-only reranking potentially poor. Natively optimized candidates may achieve lower residual while entering a prior-incompatible basin; NP scores can differ because of y-clamping, LF masks and step averaging.

**Falsification using existing artifacts only, not tuning**: For each exposed image and actual stored candidate, compute/collect already logged (a) final signed `L_y` if present, (b) native precommitted selector, (c) GT PSNR offline, and (d) per-family selection regret `max_j PSNR_j - PSNR_selected`; report bad/G25 regret and rank discordance by image and family. A null or reverse relationship between signed final residual and PSNR in DAPS/SITCOM would refute an assumption that lowest residual is universally fair. B22 SITCOM already stores a full signed residual for all four candidates; the NP external raw-signed terminal residual is **not proven stored** and cannot be recomputed from pixels without a separately authorized CPU operator evaluation, so no imaginary zero-cost matrix of common scores. Descriptive scatter/rank plots are not grounds for optimizing a new selector on these panels.

4. **Common clean-free selector**: only if separately justified, measurement preprocessing matched and evaluation prospective; include every eligible family, signed `L_y`, plus a fixed explicitly prior-sensitive feature only if principled and prospectively frozen. **Novelty partition:**
- **Algorithmic increment:** score multiple denoised noise proposals by low-frequency magnitude, propagate winning state/noise, delay projection; prior implementation is concrete but ingredients (best-of-K, diffusion denoiser, Fourier PR projection, heuristic rerank) substantially established. Moderate engineering specificity, **low-to-moderate stand-alone theory/method novelty** without controlled ablation.
- **Empirical mechanism/reliability:** failure anatomy, algorithm-specific executable selectors and candidate-generation vs selection, and cost-vs-tail tradeoff. Distinct, potentially useful when run comparably on fresh cases, but B22's three-point Good25 advantage lacks inferential support.
- **Posterior fidelity/correctness:** **unsupported for NP**; B25 exact finite-support results actually separate good posterior calibration from one-truth PSNR and prove no general reconstruction improvement. B26 is isolated and pending.

**Two honest manuscript frames:**

**A (preferred): reliability/system characterization**, e.g. *Cost- and Selector-Aware Reliability of Noise Selection for Diffusion Phase Retrieval*. Paper question: whether a frozen, executable NP multi-start policy occupies a reliable operating region at a given compute budget versus native DAPS/SITCOM restarts. Contributions: precise native-path method, preexisting failure taxonomy; prospective matched-K and matched-GPU-s reliability curves; cross-family selector/oracle decomposition; paired tail-uncertainty and reproducible accounting. This can be a short signal-processing/computational imaging contribution **only if** prospective comparisons demonstrate meaningful, nontrivial efficacy beyond baseline restarts. Without gains, a carefully negative benchmark is possibly a workshop/reproducibility report, **not automatically a journal/conference methods paper**.

The B25 `sqrt(h)` vs `h` toy finding and exact finite-support posterior-mode audits are diagnostic only; non-iid native NP, incomplete trajectory/target treatment, and adjacent TDS/MGDM/NCS theory block stronger Bayesian claims. **NO-GO as an independent main method/theory paper on existing evidence**.
