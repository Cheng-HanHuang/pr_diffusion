## 0. Executive synopsis for a future metaplanner
The initial intended contribution was a reliability and quality–efficiency trade-off from **Noise Picking (NP)** for Fourier phase retrieval with a pretrained FFHQ diffusion prior. Against DAPS and SITCOM alone, NP appeared intermediate: good high-quality recovery for substantially less cost than DAPS, better PSNR than SITCOM but slower. That was plausible preliminary positioning, not established innovation.

Our **A1.4 competitor search changed the decision**. `FAST-DIPS` (ICLR 2026) directly addresses phase retrieval with a compatible Fourier-magnitude operator and considerably better observed computational efficiency. **A1.5 actually reproduced native FAST** on the same eight B22-exposed FFHQ image/measurement pairs: FAST's offline GT-best-of-four PSNR **31.525 dB** matched DAPS **31.518 dB**, versus NP-LF **30.388 dB** and SITCOM **27.439 dB**. FAST's reported K4 costs differ in timing scope from our older external subprocess measurements, so *do not claim a precise speedup ratio*, but the overall observed FAST quality/compute performance undermines NP's original standalone Pareto narrative.

A1.6 then tested **17 NP/FAST hybrid engineering arms, 260/260 candidate runs across four GPUs**, still on those same eight exposed images. FAST correction to NP gives only **+0.184 dB oracle K4** relative to a paired NP baseline on the A1.6 seed set, dominated by one image and with no root-level catastrophic-failure reduction. Sparse two-branch+FAST keeps K4 oracle quality nearly unchanged with about **half as many U-Net evaluations**, but several ingredients were changed together and the resulting quality/compute frontier **still trails native FAST**. No demonstrated novel algorithmic gain survives as a standalone NP-method publication.

**Decision:** stop adding NP variants and refrain from a large prospective benchmark, new paper draft or another GPU sweep to rescue this architecture. Record outcomes as useful negative/reproducibility evidence. If someone presents a *different, clearly novel question*, reopen with a separate literature/identifiability gate before any new PAC commitment.

### C. A1.3 and A1.5/A1.6 physics and candidate-selection lessons
- `A(x)=|\mathcal F x|` is nonnegative (after actual frozen padding/range convention), but the saved **Gaussian-noisy magnitude** `y` can be signed. Roughly **36.6–39.4%** of measured entries were negative. Clipping to `y_+=max(y,0)` changes a *candidate-dependent* squared residual:
  `||A(x)-y||_2^2-||A(x)-y_+||_2^2 = sum_{i:y_i<0}(2A_i(x)|y_i|+y_i^2)`.
  It can reverse terminal-root rankings; preserving original signed observations is important even if an algorithm uses clipped magnitudes internally. This identity is a basic useful diagnostic, not standalone mathematical novelty.
- A1.3 on the same eight exposed images: external signed/clipped K4 terminal selection yields DAPS **28.258 / 31.493 dB** (GT oracle31.518); NP-LF **30.367 / 25.880** (oracle30.388); NP-S2 **30.203 / 25.914** (oracle30.222). This **method-specific preference reversal** means choosing DAPS-clipped and NP-signed *now* based on observed GT would be an **adaptively fitted rule**. No independent validation. Native cross-root scores are missing or incomparable; no phantom native-selector claims.
- In A1.5, original signed residual selected FAST K4 mean **31.483 dB**, GT oracle31.525, exploratory regret ~0.042dB. In A1.6, signed terminal reranking of NP LF+FAST-post yields **30.387 dB / Good25 8/8** versus clipped **23.619dB / Good255/8** (oracle30.415); beam2 signed30.153 vs clipped22.952. That promising sign separation **still uses the same exposed eight images** and is not a broadly validated clean-free method.
- The physically well-defined external residual is **not globally calibrated across methods**: pooling all 16 A1.3 terminal candidates per image and ranking by a shared residual chose SITCOM **8/8**, although DAPS supplied the highest raw-PSNR GT-oracle output on all eight. Hence *within-family candidate reranking* and *cross-family method routing* are distinct research claims.
- Fourier magnitude is invariant under conjugate reflection (with the verified data/operator conventions); a 180°-flipped candidate may be **measurement-indistinguishable but have disastrous raw-oriented PSNR**. E.g. `68539` DAPS candidate ~6.36dB raw, ~31.87dB after a **GT-aware diagnostic** 180° rotation. There is no magnitude-only guarantee to recover canonical GT orientation without additional asymmetric information/prior. **Raw-oriented PSNR remains the project’s primary practical score**; symmetry-aware PSNR is *auxiliary only*, never a surreptitious GT-free rescue.
- A1.3 retrospective taxonomy `86 RAW_GOOD25, 3 rot180-rescuable, 25 measurement-consistent low-raw, 14 unresolved, 0 flagged poor measurement fit` over 128 endpoints is **heuristic**. It is not a proof of topologically distinct basins or sampler convergence.
- A1.5 FAST M0 source-physics audit established `||A(x)-y||_2≥||min(y,0)||_2`; signed observation negative-part norms `18.566–19.569` make FAST's *nominal literal image-space global ball* with `epsilon=.05` empty on these observations. FAST's *auxiliary measurement-variable* ADMM correction need not produce an image in that ball, and finite iterations do not imply invalidity. Algebra also suggests an exact initial zero-gradient image substep under pinned initialization, but **no source-equivalent GPU causal ablation** has proved an implementational improvement. Neither is a validated FAST bug or independent publication result.

## 2. Why this particular method-paper path was parked
1. **Competitor gap:** original DAPS/SITCOM quality–efficiency compromise ceased to be a distinct publication case once a source-faithful FAST-DIPS reproduction was included. Always compare to contemporary strong native baselines, not just historical B22.
2. **Quality signal too weak:** NP FAST-post correction gives +0.184dB K4 on the *exposed* paired seed panel, dominated by one image; no robust mean-root/tail gain. A1.6 FAST-style candidate handling, sparse allocation and branching do not outrun native FAST at comparable quality.
3. **Mechanism unisolated:** sparse beam quality/cost result is useful engineering but cannot be attributed to branch persistence without controls; generic restart, correction and proposal-count reductions have substantial prior art.
4. **Reliability claim unresolved:** Good25 saturates at K4; selection diagnostics are from observed GT-exposed images, signed/clipped scores are standard physical residuals, no prospective deployable method validated, and raw orientation is unidentifiable from exact Fourier magnitude alone.
5. **Novelty and evidence gates:** prior mismatch is well studied; failure-detection literature includes Xue et al. ISCAS2025 whose full algorithm remained **unverified**. There is no genuinely independent pristine validation image cohort for this claim. Continuing a large tuning search on exposed GT without a clear publishable mechanism risks compounding selection bias.

**Consequence:** this is a **scientific STOP on the current NP standalone paper**, *not* a negative theorem ruling out all noise proposal selection, all reliability research, or Track B's basin-transition approach. Do not recycle 260 candidate roots as 260 independent images, treat an oracle as executable, mix seed populations or hide source-runtime differences.
